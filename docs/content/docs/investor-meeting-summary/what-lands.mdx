title: What lands in Airtable
description: The note format, the twelve sections, and the fields the form sets.
---

One new note record, linked to the firm. Nothing that already exists is edited or
overwritten.

## It is filed through the form, on purpose

The plugin submits the **New meeting or firm updates** form rather than creating
the row directly through the API. That distinction matters more than it sounds.

Two automations in the base trigger on *form submitted*, not on *record created*.
One of them tags you as the **Portal Contact** on everyone you met with — which is
how the base records who at Portal owns that relationship, and what the form's own
helper text promises. A row created through the API looks perfect and silently
skips it, so nobody gets tagged and nothing signals that anything went wrong.

Submitting the form also triggers everything a created record would, so nothing is
lost by doing it this way.

## The note

Twelve possible sections. **A section appears only if the conversation actually
produced it** — most notes use four or five. A heading with "not discussed"
underneath is worse than no heading.

1. Since last time
2. Key highlights
3. Fund and thesis
4. Companies they are working on
5. Companies they are sharing with us
6. Companies we are sharing with them
7. Passes and negatives
8. Their process
9. Co-investors they named
10. People and personal
11. Follow-ups
12. Next contact

Sections 4, 5 and 6 stay separate deliberately. They are three different
obligations, and collapsing them into one "companies discussed" list loses which
one you owe.

Bullets only, no prose blocks. There is no word limit — a cap just makes facts get
dropped to fit — but notes land naturally between about 150 and 350 words.

## What is left out

Nothing about Portal itself: our fundraising, our metrics, our product. Three
exceptions, because each is information about the investor:

- commitments the investor made to us;
- their reaction to something of ours, where the reaction *is* the point
  ("the New Jersey angle is what interests them");
- a company we committed to send them.

The filter is relevance to the investor, not caution. It is never a reason to
write a thinner note about something sensitive.

## Example

> **Redpine Ventures, 2026-08-26. Dana Whitfield (Partner). Meeting.**
>
> **Key highlights**
> - Fund III is closing early next year and Dana said they will be writing bigger first checks out of it.
> - They are actively looking at ADC platforms following two portfolio exits.
>
> **Fund and thesis**
> - First checks moving up from roughly $1M–$2M to $3M–$5M.
> - Stays at seed and Series A, does not follow into B.
> - Invests nationally, and Dana was specific that non-coastal is not a barrier.
>
> **Companies they are sharing with us**
> - Dana offered to send two oncology startups once she has cleared it with the founders.
>
> **Follow-ups**
> - Ignacio: send the non-confidential summary on the cardiovascular device company.
> - Dana: make the co-investor introduction.

Eight of the twelve sections appeared in the full version of that note. The other
four are absent because that conversation did not produce them. That is the
correct output, not an incomplete one.

## The fields it sets

| Field | What goes in |
| --- | --- |
| Submitted by | You, plus any colleagues who were on the call |
| Firm | The one firm record, after you confirm it |
| Your contacts | The investor-side people, linked when they are already in the CRM |
| Update type | Meeting, or non-meeting update |
| Notes | The note above |
| Notes cateogry | Tagged from the four existing options — never a new one |

Everything else on the form is optional and only set if you ask for it.
