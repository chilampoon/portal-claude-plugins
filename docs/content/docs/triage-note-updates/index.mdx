## Finishing a triage

A triage ends with a decision, and the decision is what moves the company. This
takes the note you already wrote and makes the record say so.

<Steps>
<Step n="01" title="Hand Claude the note">

Attach the Word doc or PDF, or paste the email in, and say:

<Say>Here are my triage notes on Marrowlight Bio — we're passing.</Say>

Several people's notes on the same company at once is fine. Say so, and Claude
merges them while keeping each person's contribution attributable.

Claude keeps your structure. It tightens the wording; it does not reorganise your
note into a template, and it does not add analysis you did not write.

</Step>
<Step n="02" title="Read the proposal">

There is no fixed field map. Claude reads the table's live schema and proposes
where each piece of your note belongs, as a table: the field, whether it is
appending or replacing, and the exact text going in. Appending is the default —
replacing takes its own separate yes.

Edit the proposal, or approve it. Nothing is written until you do.

It also shows you the Startup record it matched, with the company's current
pipeline stage, so you can catch a near-duplicate before anything lands.

</Step>
<Step n="03" title="Confirm the stage move on its own">

The stage change is confirmed separately from everything else, showing the old
value and the new one. Claude will remind you every time that moving this field
fires Airtable automations — it writes a Stage History row and emails the venture
team.

If the recommendation in your note is ambiguous, or you did not write one, Claude
asks which stage you want. It never picks one for you.

</Step>
<Step n="04" title="Approve the reply to the fellows">

Moving the stage makes Airtable email the venture fellows, asking whoever moved
it to reply-all with why. Claude waits for that email to land, checks the stages
in it match what it just wrote, and drafts the reply using the same sentences that
went onto the record — so the two cannot drift.

It shows you the draft and everyone on it, and sends only when you say so. That is
its own separate yes, because the reply reaches the whole fellows list. Without a
mail connector, or if the notification has not turned up, Claude hands you the text
to send yourself and tells you which it is.

The thread your note came in on — where a senior associate forwarded the company —
is never touched.

</Step>
<Step n="05" title="Let Diligence support know">

Any run that writes also sends a short changelog to the people named on the
record's Diligence support field — the company, a link to the record, the stage
move if there was one, and a line for each field that changed. Claude resolves
the recipients off the record and shows you who they are; as with the reply,
nothing goes out until you say so.

</Step>
<Step n="06" title="Take what comes back">

A link to the updated record, whichever emails you had Claude send, and a list of
any images from your note, because Claude cannot upload those for you.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Your decision sets the size of the write

- **Meet, or advance to diligence** — the full treatment. Everything in the note
  that fits a field gets proposed.
- **Pass, or soft pass** — deliberately less. The reason for the move, the risks
  written as what would have to change for us to re-engage, your full note
  archived in **Notes**, and the stage and date. Nobody reads a teardown on a
  dead company.
- **Ambiguous, or no recommendation** — Claude asks rather than guessing.

## What lands in Airtable

The company's record in the Startups table, which is what the **Deals** interface
opens when you click the company in Deal Flow. Text that is already there stays
exactly as it is; yours goes underneath, under a dated header. Below is the
shape of a **pass** — the short version.

### Where things usually land

These pairings are settled — content of each kind goes to the field beside it,
and a section that has a home here does not get quietly skipped. The list is not
the whole table, though: for anything else in your note, Claude reads the live
schema and proposes what fits.

**Secret Sauce** is the exception — the one field Claude works out rather than
files. It asks what this company has that the others do not, usually the
strongest of the strengths fused with something specific about the technology,
and it is reasoned from your note alone. If your note does not support a real
claim of differentiation, Claude leaves it empty and tells you, rather than
restating the strengths under a second heading.

## Good to know

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Nothing is written without your yes">You see every field it will touch, and whether each is an append or a replace, before anything lands.</Fact>
  <Fact title="The stage move is a separate yes">It is shown as old → new. Claude tells you each time that the write sets off Airtable automations — a Stage History row, and the email to the fellows. Airtable treats the write like a manual edit, so Claude can neither send that email nor stop it. Moving a company to <strong>0.0 - Outside of Core Geographies</strong> is flagged on its own, because it can email outside the team.</Fact>
  <Fact title="The reply needs its own yes">Nothing goes out until you have seen the draft and the full recipient list. It is a reply-all to every venture fellow, so it never rides along on an earlier approval.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Diligence support hears about it">Every run that writes sends them a changelog — the record, the stage move, and a line per field. Recipients are looked up in Airtable down to their actual email addresses, never guessed from a name, and you approve them before it sends.</Fact>
  <Fact title="It reads a field before writing to it">A field's name is not its definition. <strong>Origin</strong> records where the deal came from, not where your note came from, so Claude checks what is already in a field before proposing it — and never puts filenames or run summaries on the record.</Fact>
  <Fact title="What it writes has a shape">Questions come back grouped by subheading and numbered one per line, so you can work down them in front of the company. Every other field leads with the point, bullets the evidence, and closes with the takeaway.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Claude signs what Claude wrote">Anything it composed for you ends with <em>(summarized by Claude)</em> and the model that wrote it. Your own words — including the full note archived into Notes — are never signed that way.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Nothing existing is replaced by default">It reads each field first and appends underneath. If it cannot read a field, it stops rather than writing over it.</Fact>
  <Fact title="It files notes, it does not write them">Nothing goes in a field unless your note supports it. Hand it nothing and it asks for your note.</Fact>
  <Fact title="You have to upload the images yourself">Claude has no way to put an attachment into Airtable, so it lists the figures it found and leaves them to you.</Fact>
  <Fact title="It never creates a startup record">Add the company through the new-startup form first.</Fact>
</Facts>
